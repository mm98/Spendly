14a Input types
Every control used across the set, with its states and what is specific about it here. This is the whole vocabulary — a screen needing something that isn't on this page is a signal to reconsider the screen.
NOTES FOR IMPLEMENTATION
One box, everywhere. Every text-entry and select control is 1px solid #dedcd4, radius 9px, padding 10px 12px, text 14px — landing at 42px tall. Controls that aren't text, like the stepper, get that height explicitly rather than sized by content, so a row of mixed controls needs no alignment work.
Focus is one treatment — border #1d4d33 plus a 3px rgba(29,77,51,0.12) ring, on every focusable control including toggles, checkboxes and drop zones.
Labels always visible — 12px/600 above the control. No floating labels, no placeholder-as-label: both lose the label when the user is typing and needs it.
Validation on blur once touched, and on submit. Never per keystroke. An error clears on the next valid blur, not the first character typed.
Touch — 42px is under the 44px minimum, so on mobile vertical padding goes to 12px and every control's row carries 44px of hit area.

Three groups, in this order: Favourites, Recent, then everything else. Group headers are labels, not options — never focusable, never selectable, and they disappear when their group has no matches. The count on the last header is the count after filtering.
Favourites are explicit, Recent is automatic. A vendor is starred by the user (from the library, not from here); Recent is the last three used, excluding anything already starred so nothing appears twice. Cap Favourites at five — beyond that it is just a second list.
Arrow keys cross group boundaries as one flat list. The visual grouping must not affect traversal: Down from the last favourite lands on the first recent item, skipping the header.
Matches are highlighted, and matching is accent-insensitive on word prefixes — mob finds Møbler, and it matches inside a hyphenated or compound name.
"Create X" is always last, quoting exactly what was typed, and it is reachable by keyboard like any other row. It is the only way a vendor is created during data entry — name only, the rest filled in later from the library. A required-field form inside a dropdown stops the user mid-task.
Nothing is selected by typing alone. No inline autocomplete into the field, no selection on blur — the user commits with Enter or a click. Silent selection is how the wrong vendor ends up on an order.

Icon before the label, never after. Icon-only buttons are 32px in a row cluster, 28px inside an item line, and every one carries an aria-label and a tooltip.
One primary per view. Two primaries means neither is. On the edit screen it is Save; Duplicate and Delete drop a level even though they matter.
Destructive is not a red primary. It only exists inside a confirm dialog, so a red button on a page is always a bug — the page-level Delete is a secondary that opens the dialog.
Disabled is a last resort. Prefer letting the click happen and showing what is wrong: a disabled Save with no explanation is the most common dead end in a form. Where it is unavoidable, the tooltip says what would enable it.
Loading keeps the width and swaps the label for a spinner plus a verb ("Saving…"). No layout shift, and the button stays disabled for the duration so a double-click can't double-submit.
Labels are verbs, and specific — "Import 41 orders", not "Confirm". A button that names its consequence needs no explanatory sentence beside it.
Geometry: radius 9px, 13px/600 text, padding 10px 17px primary / 10px 16px secondary (the 1px border keeps the box identical) / 10px 12px ghost. Small variant is 12px text at 8px 13px. Never full-width on desktop; always full-width in a mobile sheet.

14c Extending the palette
The seven picker colours aren't a selection of nice greens — they sit on a rule, and the rule is what lets an eighth be added without a designer. Values are OKLCH, because the rule is only visible in a perceptual space: in hex these look arbitrary, in OKLCH they line up.
THE RULE, IN THREE PARTS
1 — Four hue families, and nothing between them

The neutrals are the quiet part of the system: they sit on the amber hue at almost no chroma. That is why the canvas reads as warm greige rather than grey, and why a true grey dropped in anywhere looks dirty beside it.
2 — Chroma falls as lightness rises

Lightness climbs in even perceptual steps and chroma drops with it. That is the whole ramp: a light colour in this system is never as saturated as a dark one. Hold chroma flat while lightening and the pale end turns to mint — the single most common way a palette like this gets broken.
3 — Chroma is capped
Nothing in the app exceeds C 0.12, and that ceiling belongs to one colour — the amber marker at #b58a2b, which has to read as a 2px hairline. Picker swatches stop at C 0.08. This is why the interface never looks like a dashboard: saturation is spent on a single marker rather than distributed across every chart.
ADDING AN EIGHTH PICKER COLOUR
1
Pick the hue: an existing family, or a new one at least 40° from all four. Anything closer reads as a mistake rather than a distinction.
2
Set lightness in L 69–86, the band the existing swatches occupy, and keep at least 5 L from every swatch on the same hue — closer than that and the two are indistinguishable in a 12px chip.
3
Derive chroma from that lightness rather than choosing it by eye: roughly 0.08 at L 70, 0.05 at L 80, 0.025 at L 86. Never above 0.08.
4
Check the four places it lands: a 26px icon tile with a white glyph, the same tile with dark ink (above L 75 the glyph flips), a 12px chart bar between its neighbours, and a filter chip.
5
Add the dark-theme pair in the same commit. Dark lifts L by about +18 and holds chroma; a swatch without its pair only works in one theme.
WORKED EXAMPLE
A blue is wanted for "Electronics". Nearest hue is green at 150, so H 250 clears the 40° rule. Place it mid-band at L 74, which sets chroma at about 0.07.

WHAT NOT TO DO
Don't open a free colour picker to users. Every swatch has to survive four contexts and a second theme, and a hex chosen from a wheel satisfies none of them — this is decision 5b in the design, not a limitation to be relaxed later.
Don't add a colour to distinguish two things that a label already distinguishes. Twelve categories don't need twelve colours; the top five plus "other" is what the charts show, and that is deliberate.
Don't interpolate in hex or HSL when generating variants. HSL lightness is not perceptual, and the midpoints come out muddy — the whole reason this page is written in OKLCH.
14d Component API — the options each control needs
What each control must accept to serve every place it is used in this design, and nothing beyond that. Thirteen components. The right-hand column names what drives each option, so an option with nothing behind it can be cut.
NOTES FOR IMPLEMENTATION
Every control takes the same five: label, value, onChange, error, disabled — plus id, name and required passed through. Don't re-declare them per component. One shared field wrapper owns the label, the error message and the spacing; each control renders only its own box.
Controlled only. No internal value state, no defaultValue. The order draft is the single source of truth — a control keeping its own copy is how a field silently disagrees with what gets saved.
No variant="danger" on an input. Error is a boolean plus a message, never a visual variant, which makes the red state impossible to apply for decoration.
Resist the extra option. Each row is short on purpose. When a screen needs something not listed, add it here first — that review step is what keeps this at thirteen components rather than forty.

THE SHARED FIELD WRAPPER
One component above all of them, owning what every field has in common. Build this first — it is the reason none of the controls below carry a label prop of their own.
Order date
the control renders only this box
hint, or the error message, in the same slot
It owns: the label and its htmlFor, the required marker, the hint/error slot and its aria-describedby, aria-invalid, and the 5px label gap. Error and hint share one slot so a field never grows when it fails — the row height is identical in both states, which is what stops a form jumping as you tab through it.
WHAT IS NOT A COMPONENT HERE
Radio. Never used on its own — every either/or in this design is a Segmented or a Combobox. Don't build one until a screen needs it.
Slider. No slider anywhere. An amount range in the facets is two AmountFields, because a receipt total is typed, not dragged to.
Autocomplete, tag input, rich text, native select. All are the Combobox, the Chip list, the multiline TextField, or nothing. Each would be a second implementation of something already here, and the second one is always the one that misses the keyboard support.
Form. No form component. Screens compose the wrapper and the controls directly, because each screen's save behaviour differs — per card in Settings, one sticky bar on order edit, a staged dry run in import.
BUILD ORDER
1. The field wrapper, then TextField and Button — between them they unblock every screen.
2. Combobox. The largest piece by a distance, and four screens are waiting on it. Do the keyboard table in 14a before the styling, not after.
3. AmountField and Stepper, together — they share the parsing and formatting.
4. Checkbox, Segmented, Chip, SearchInput. Small, and the order list needs all four at once.
5. FileDrop, DateField, SwatchPicker, Toggle.
Each one lands with its states from this page as the acceptance criteria — default, filled, focus, error, read-only, disabled, loading where it applies. A control shipped without its error state gets that state invented three times later, differently.
