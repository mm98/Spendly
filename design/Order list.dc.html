    Notes for implementation
    The range appears above the list as one chip, like any other active filter. Removing it returns the Date group to its presets.
    The thumbnail keeps a 1px --line border, inset. Product photography is mostly shot on white, so without it a pale photo has no edge against the white row and the cell reads as empty. box-sizing: border-box - the border comes out of the 42px, it does not grow the thumb.
    The extra-photo count is a badge on the thumbnail, bottom-right, rgba(27, 26, 23, 0.72) with white 9px/600 - the same treatment as the card strip's +N. It used to sit in the gutter beside the thumb inside a 58px box that existed only to reserve space for an optional element; a count floating between two columns belongs to neither. On the thumb the cell is a flat 42px, so the vendor name starts at the same x whether or not a count exists.
    A preset stores a token, not the dates it resolves to. The four named periods re-evaluate on every load, so a saved view called "This quarter" shows Q3 in September and Q4 in October. Only Custom… holds absolute dates. A view that stored 2026-07-01 → 2026-09-30 when the user picked "This quarter" is frozen to that quarter forever and gives no sign it has stopped rolling - store the period and resolve at query time. Week starts per the Settings value; quarters are calendar quarters.
    One control, four settings. The presets and the range are the same facet: picking a preset clears the range, setting a range deselects the presets. They cannot both be active.
    The thumbnail cell has exactly three states, and one width. A 42px (--mark-medium) rounded square holding the first item photo; the same square 1px dashed --line-3 on --surface-2 when the order has no photo at all; and either of those followed by a +N count at 11px/600 --ink-4 when more photos exist. The cell is 58px in every case, so the vendor name starts at the same x on every row. Nothing else goes here - no stacked pairs, no ring, no second image.
    The diagonal hatch is not a state. It is how these mockups draw a photo they do not have. Anywhere you see it, a real image renders - object-fit: cover, centred. Only the dashed square means "no photo", and it is the one treatment that survives into the build as drawn.
    The count is photos, the paperclip is attachments. They are different things and can disagree: an order can have four item photos and no receipt, or a receipt and no photos. The paperclip sits after the reference line and appears only when a file is attached to the order itself.
    Draft and Cancelled share the grey pill; the row treatment separates them. They are not two points on one scale - a draft is not finished yet, a cancelled order no longer counts - so a sixth colour would wrongly imply they are comparable. A cancelled row renders every cell at opacity 0.6 with the amount struck through in --ink-5; the pill stays at full contrast, because the label is what distinguishes the two and dimming it would make the state harder to read. Draft rows are entirely normal. Opacity is reinforcement, never the signal - the word is the signal, which is what keeps it working for anyone who cannot separate the tints.
    The strikethrough answers a second question. Cancelled orders are excluded from total spend, so a struck amount states that in the row without a legend. The not counted line beneath replaces the saved-percentage line, which has no meaning on an order that did not happen.
    No Apply button. It filters as soon as a valid date is entered, because every other facet applies immediately. From alone means "from that date to today", which is why To carries Today as its placeholder rather than blocking.
    Inline, not a popover. A calendar floating over the list hides the results the range is changing. Each field opens the platform date picker on click and accepts typing in the Settings date format.
    An invalid range does not clear the list. The field goes red and the results hold at the last valid range - recomputing to zero results while someone is mid-edit reads as "no orders exist".
    Both dates are in the URL, so a custom range is linkable and survives reload like every other facet.

    Flyouts open to the left, because the menu is already right-aligned against the row's end. They open on hover after 120ms, on click, and on → or Enter from the keyboard; ← or Esc returns to the parent menu with the item still highlighted.
    One flyout at a time. Moving to another parent item closes the open one immediately - no two-level cascade anywhere in this app, and a submenu never has a submenu.
    Single-row edits apply immediately and offer Undo; bulk edits confirm. That is the difference between the flyouts here and the popovers in 2a, and it follows the rule that undo is preferred over confirmation where the action is cheap to reverse.
    Both flyouts are the Combobox from 14a in its two modes - pick-only for status, searchable multi-select for category. They are not new components, and they inherit its keyboard table and its flat traversal.

    Notes for implementation
    Photos in the strip carry no border of their own in the light theme. In dark mode each keeps the 1px --line-3 edge every thumbnail has there (17b), drawn inside the photo so the strip does not grow. The strip is a continuous band with one 1px --line edge along its bottom, and the hairlines between photos are the 1px gaps letting the --bg band through. A border per photo would put a second line beside every gap and turn the band into a grid of framed boxes. This is the opposite of the 42px row thumbnail in 2b, which is a lone object on white and does need its own edge - the difference is that a strip already has a boundary and a thumbnail does not.
    The strip carries one hairline, along its bottom. --line, the same token as the card's own border, so the band closes against the card body instead of bleeding into it. Individual photos get no border here - that is the difference from a 42px row thumbnail, where the photo sits directly on white and needs its own edge. In the strip the --surface-3 band is already the edge, and bordering each photo would put back the cell furniture the single-surface strip exists to avoid.
    The card menu is the row menu. Same six items in the same order - Open order, Duplicate, Download receipt, Set status ›, Add category ›, Delete - because the view toggle changes how an order is drawn, not what can be done to it. A card that offered a different set would mean the toggle silently changes capability, and a user who found Duplicate in rows would go looking for it here.
    It anchors to the card, not the viewport. Bottom-right above the footer line, flipping upward when the card is near the bottom of the grid. The ⋮ is always visible at --ink-6 - never hover-revealed, for the same reason as the row (16b): an affordance that needs a cursor does not exist on touch.
    Why it sits on the footer line rather than over the photo. A control over the photo strip would land on a photo of unknown lightness and compete with the checkbox and the paperclip badge, which are already there. On the footer it aligns with the amount above it - 44px target, glyph flush at the card's 14px padding edge.
    What is visible when. Resting, a card shows no checkbox - the strip is the whole top edge and a permanent tick box on every card would compete with the photos for the one thing the view exists to show. Hovering a card reveals its checkbox in the strip's top-left, with its own white fill and a soft shadow, so it reads against any photo; nothing is drawn behind it. Once anything on the page is selected, every card shows its checkbox permanently, ticked or not, because at that point the page is in a selecting mood and a hidden target is the wrong affordance. Clearing the last selection returns them all to hover-only. On touch, where there is no hover, the checkboxes are permanent. On the No photos band it has no shadow.
    The bulk bar behaves identically to rows. It is absent at rest, appears above the grid the moment the first card is ticked, and disappears when the last is cleared. Same four actions, same order, same height. In rows it replaces the summary line because both occupy the toolbar; here the grid has no toolbar, so the bar is inserted above it as another child of the same 14px column - the container owns the spacing, and the bar carries no margin of its own. That is the one difference between the two views, and it is a consequence of the layout rather than a decision.
    A selected card takes a 2px --green border in place of its 1px --line hairline - the card's own edge carries the state, since 2h's --green-tint row tint would fight the photo strip. Keep the border on the same box so nothing reflows: 2px border with 1px less padding, not an added outline.
    Every overlay on a photo carries a shadow - 0 1px 4px rgba(27, 26, 23, 0.28) on the checkbox in both states and on the paperclip badge. The photos are the user's own, so the lightness behind an overlay is never knowable: a white control on a pale product shot disappears, and the filled green one disappears on a dark photo. The shadow costs nothing where the photo already contrasts and saves the case where it does not - the same reason 12a keeps a border on every thumbnail.
    The strip is one surface, not a row of boxes. A single --bg band, photos filling it edge to edge, separated only by a 1px gap that lets the tint show through as a hairline - no padding, no per-photo cell, no border. Product photography is mostly shot on white, so the tint is what gives a pale photo an edge; dividing it into cells adds the edges back as furniture and turns a two-photo card into one image beside an empty panel that reads as a failed load.
    Cells divide by the number of photos, never by three. One photo fills the strip, two halve it, three third it. There is no such thing as an empty cell.
    No photos still renders the band - --surface-3 with a centred image glyph in --ink-6 and the label No photos. Dropping it entirely would make cards different heights in the grid, and an order with nothing photographed is a normal order, not a broken one.
    Selection survives the view toggle in both directions, and so does its count. The toggle changes how rows are drawn, not which ones exist. The select-all box leads the summary line over the grid, and the bulk bar once a card is picked, with the rows' three states: empty, dash and tick. It reaches the page only; Select all N matching is the separate step it is in rows. It sits at the same place in both bars, so nothing moves when they swap.
    Item photos, not attachments. The strip is the photo on each item line. The receipt PDF and anything else attached to the order never appear here - a scan of a receipt is not a picture of what was bought, and cards full of grey paper are useless to browse.
    Up to three, in item order. Item lines without a photo are skipped, and the count in +N is the remaining photos, not the remaining items - so an order of seven items with five photos shows three and +2. Below four photos there is no pill.
    Fewer photos means wider ones. The cells are equal fractions of a fixed 116px strip, so one photo fills the width and two split it. No empty slots, and no letterboxing to keep three columns.
    No photos is a filled state, not a gap. The strip keeps its height so the grid stays even, and holds a quiet placeholder on --surface-3. It is not a call to action: photos are added on the item, and offering an upload from a list card would put a file picker where the user is scanning.
    Photos are cropped, never letterboxed - object-fit: cover, centred. Receipt photography is rarely square and a fitted image leaves bars that read as broken.

    Notes for implementation
    A view filtered to exactly one category borrows that category's IdentityMark, tile and colour intact - the only coloured mark in the sidebar. Everywhere else the glyph is a stroke in the row's own ink, so it sits with Home and Orders rather than competing with them.
    Search text and sort never affect it - a view named for a query is still a saved search, and the bookmark says so.
    The card has two modes, and one slot. At rest it is read-only - names, icons, counts, no handles - because opening it is nearly always to switch view, not reorganise. The header slot holds Edit; when the current filters differ from the active view it holds Save instead, since saving concerns the state you are in now and outranks reorganising. In edit mode it holds Done. Never two of them at once. 9e draws both modes.
    Handles and the per-row menu exist only in edit mode. Reordering still saves on drop - Done leaves the mode, it is not a save button. All orders renders as locked rather than disappearing, so the list does not reflow when the mode changes.
    Every default view must be reproducible from the facets. The two that ship with a new account are ordinary saved views, not built-in navigation: Awaiting delivery is Status = Ordered, Missing receipt is Receipt = Missing. If a default cannot be rebuilt by a user working the rail, it is a hardcoded list pretending to be a view - and a user who deletes it has no way to recreate it.
    A saved view lands at position 2 - directly under All orders, at the top of the saved list, in both the rail and the sidebar. Appending to the end would drop it into the sidebar's overflow on any account that already has four views, so saving would appear to do nothing. It stays where it lands until dragged; nothing re-sorts on its own. 9e draws the result in both surfaces.
    A view stores the URL, not a result set. Facets, search text, sort and view mode - the same state the URL already carries, which is why a view is also just a link. Selecting one replaces the URL; nothing is cached and the counts are live.
    Naming is inline, in the position the row will occupy. The field takes focus with the count already showing, so you can see how many orders you are naming. Enter saves, Esc cancels, and the header action becomes Cancel while the field is open. Blur cancels - an empty name is never saved.
    The default name is empty, not generated. A suggested "Hay · Ordered · this month" would be accepted unread, and a rail full of filter strings is no easier to scan than the filters themselves. The active filters are shown as a line below the field instead, so the name can be typed with them in view.
    Duplicate names are allowed. A view is a personal bookmark; refusing a repeated name costs more than it prevents.
    Changing a filter while a view is selected does not silently update it. The row keeps the view name, turns amber, and the card offers Update or Revert. The state is the row's own fill rather than a badge beside the name: a chip would make that one row taller than the rest and shift the list every time a filter moved. The list follows the new filters immediately - the chip records that the saved definition and what is on screen have diverged.
    All orders is not a saved view and has no menu. It is the unfiltered list, always first, and cannot be renamed, reordered or deleted. Making a saved view the default changes what Orders opens to; All orders stays where it is. On the view that is the default, the menu says Stop opening with it in Make default's place, so Orders can open on All orders again.
    Deleting confirms, per 6d - a dialog naming the view. It is not undoable via toast, because a deleted view leaves nothing on screen to undo from.
    Counts are the live result count for each view's filters, fetched with the list. If a count is not yet known the row shows nothing there rather than a zero - a zero that turns out to be 31 is worse than a brief absence.

    THE FOUR ROW FILLS
    Rest #ffffff The card surface. A row has no fill of its own.
    Hover #f0efe9 Warm grey - under the cursor.

    Notes for implementation
    The bulk bar sits on the table's grid, not its own. Its checkbox occupies the same 18px cell as a row's, so "2 selected" starts at the same x as the column header and the thumbnails below it - one vertical edge down the whole table. The cell is the checkbox's own width: padded out to 34px it left 16px of dead space, and the label read as adrift from the control it belongs to. Laying the bar out as a plain flex row instead misaligns it from the header, which is visible precisely because the two are adjacent.
    Selection outranks hover, and the two never mix. Hovering a selected row deepens the green rather than replacing it with grey - a selected row that turns grey under the cursor looks deselected at the moment you are about to act on it. Hue carries the meaning (green chosen, grey pointed at), lightness carries the state within a hue.
    The same four apply to every list in the app - order rows and cards, vendors, categories, statuses, item lines, saved views. A list that invents its own hover value is a bug. Transition 120ms ease on background-color only; on dark the same --hover, --green-tint and --shell-current roles take their dark values, which lift rather than deepen.
    One strip, two states, swapped in place. Same height (both land at 40px inner), same position, same border below. Do not animate the swap and do not stack the bulk bar above the summary - a list that shifts down when you tick a row makes the next row you wanted move under the cursor.
    Only the toolbar changes. The column header never does. Its fill, type and hairline are identical in all four states here and identical to 2b - no fill, an --line hairline below, 11px 0.1em in --ink-5. The column header labels the columns; it says nothing about selection, so nothing about selection may change it. There are exactly three signals that rows are selected - the row tint, the checkbox state, the toolbar swap - and tinting the header adds a fourth that competes with them.
    The header checkbox has three states, driven by the page only: empty when none of the 25 rows are selected, dash when some are, tick when all are. Clicking it when empty or dashed selects the page; clicking it when ticked clears the page. That is the whole behaviour - it never reaches other pages.
    "Select all 128 matching" is a separate, explicit action, offered in a strip below the bulk bar only once the page is fully selected. It selects everything the current filter matches. Clearing any part of it drops back to a page-level selection.
    Selected rows are tinted --green-tint - lighter than the --hover hover, so hovering a selected row still reads as hovered. Selection is not a hover state and the two must be distinguishable when they coincide.
    Selection is per page and clears on any filter, search, sort or page change, with no warning - the selection no longer describes anything on screen. It is deliberately not in the URL: a shared link should not carry someone else's pending bulk action.
    Shift-click extends from the last ticked row to the clicked one. The checkbox cell stops propagation, so ticking never opens the order.
    Cards select the same way rows do. The box sits on the card's photo strip and the bulk bar takes the same place above the grid, so a selection survives the toggle in 2c in both directions. Cards have the same select-all box, leading the line above the grid.

    Four designs, each drawn at its own width, so nothing has to be inferred from a rule. Columns are dropped whole - never narrowed until their content wraps. Every text cell truncates with an ellipsis; nothing in a row is allowed to reach a second line.

    list column rest window − whichever of the above are present
    Which is why the rail collapsing is not a small change. At a 1280px window with everything present the column is 652px - narrower than the CARDS frame's own breakpoint. Send the rail to a drawer and the same window gives 936px, enough for CATEGORY DROPS. One decision about the rail moves the table two breakpoints. So do not wire these numbers to the viewport. A container query on the list column is correct at any window width and survives the rail opening, the sidebar collapsing and the panel appearing. A media query is right exactly once, for one configuration, and silently wrong for the rest.

    Notes for implementation
    A column is a header cell and a body cell, dropped together as one unit. Removing the header while the rows still render the cell puts every cell after it one place to the left, and leaves the row with more children than the grid has columns - the surplus wraps to a second line, which is what a ⋮ appearing underneath a row means. It is not a wrapping bug in the menu; it is a column-count mismatch upstream of it. Write the drop as one conditional covering both, never two.
    The check is arithmetic, not visual. Count the header's children and every row's children at each breakpoint and require them equal. A row that is suddenly twice as tall is the symptom; the count is the cause, and it is the only reliable way to catch a cell landing in the wrong column when both happen to hold short text.
    A chip's width follows its label, and that is intended. Decor, Seating and Lighting come out 52, 61 and 62px wide; they are left-aligned in the cell, so their left edges stack and only their right edges differ. Do not force them to a common width - a fixed-width chip either truncates a long category name or pads a short one out with dead space, and the category column is fluid precisely so several chips can sit in it. What must be identical is the chip itself: 2px 8px, radius 6px, 22px tall, 11px text, matching 2b and every other chip in the app.
    Container queries on the list column, not media queries on the viewport. The same table sits beside a 260px facet rail and, at 1280, without one - the widths above are the table's width, not the window's. Measuring the window gets both cases wrong.
    One grid definition, shared by the header and every row, declared once as a custom property and overridden per breakpoint. Repeating it per row is how the header drifts out of alignment the first time a column changes.
    Drop columns with display: none, and remove them from the template in the same rule. A hidden cell that still occupies a track leaves a gap the eye reads as a missing value.
    Every fluid cell needs min-width: 0 and every text cell overflow: hidden; text-overflow: ellipsis; white-space: nowrap. Without the first, a grid child refuses to shrink below its content and the row overflows instead of truncating - the same automatic-minimum-size rule as 13a and 9g. This is what produces wrapping and overlap at narrow widths.
    Amount, date and status never wrap and never truncate - they are short, fixed-width, and a truncated amount is worse than no amount. Give them white-space: nowrap and a width that fits the longest value the format can produce, not the longest in the sample data.
    The category chip truncates inside its own cell rather than pushing the row. It is the only variable-length cell besides vendor.
