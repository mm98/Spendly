Notes for implementation
A share row is named by the words it draws. The row that opens a category's or a vendor's orders takes no aria-label: its name is the name, the amount and the share as drawn ("Transport 4.795 kr. 18 %").
Median order, not average. The order figure and its sparkline are the median of the orders in the set, so one very large order barely moves them. Total spend and the breakdowns are still sums. The comparison pill compares median with median.
Include N left-out orders appears only when the period holds at least one order marked Leave out of statistics (1d); N is that count, singular for one. Off by default. Turning it on puts them back into every figure on the page for this visit; it is in the URL as excluded=include and is not remembered. The switch says nothing when the period has none, so it is absent rather than disabled. On a phone it sits under the period select (Mobile screens, Statistics), with the same rules. When every order in the set is left out (a vendor whose default leaves its orders out, 5l), the page is its empty state reading "All 12 orders here are left out of statistics", with the Include switch above it as the way to see them.
Every card follows the period, and none is tied to a month. By category, Top vendors and Limits cover the whole period, like the figures, the charts and the order list (here 2026 to date; 4g is the same page for one month). Limits are set per month, so for a longer period a category's limit is its monthly limit times the months in the period: 12 for a year, 3 for a quarter; a custom period counts each month by the share of its days it covers. The pace marker is how far through the period today is, by days (day 232 of 365 is 64%, drawn at the nearest step of 5). The card's name is Limits for every period, Month included.
An order counts its full total in every category it carries. Categories overlap (Fastfood is a kind of Food), so a 194,50 kr. order in Fastfood and Food is 194,50 kr. in each, never 97,25 kr. Inside a category crumb, Spend, Median order, the limit, the charts and the head of "The N orders behind this" all use the orders' full totals, so the head's sum is the sum of the Total column: 841,22 kr. for the four orders in Fastfood, October. Category figures are therefore not parts of one whole and can add up to more than the period's spend. A vendor still counts once, since an order has one vendor. In the app this replaces the / ot.category_count share in api/src/lib/stats.js and api/src/lib/aggregate.js.
By category is the ranked row list, as Top vendors draws it (4a, 4g, and inside a crumb 4b): each category's full amount and its order count, the bar scaled to the largest category in the list. The order count after the amount is the row meta column (sp-row-meta), as in Top vendors, Limits (sp-row-meta--limit) and By status (sp-row-meta--share), so the amounts in a card line up. It carries no percentage, no ring, no Other row and no total line, since the categories do not add up to the period's spend. Inside a category crumb the Spend figure has no share line, and the slice's order list head has no "% of" meta. A vendor's share of a period is kept ("29% of the year"), because a vendor counts once.
Stacked counts each order once, under its first category, the first one on the order as it is listed on the order page, so a bar's segments always add up to the bar. The segments are the top 3 categories of By category; an order whose first category is not one of them goes in Other, as does an order with none. This is the one place an order is not counted in full in every category: By category, Limits and a category crumb use the full amount, so a Stacked segment can be smaller than that category's row.
The four stats fold to two by two below a 996px row (sp-stats--fold): four of 240 and three gaps. The row is 998 in a 1280 shell, so it folds as soon as the shell is narrower. It is always four across or two, never three over one. On a phone every stats row is two by two.
The period bar wraps rather than squeezing. The sizes keep their full width at every desktop width. When the row has no room for everything, the window, the resolved range and the compare switch move to a second line, in that order. The day count is hidden below 1100. Below 640 the bar is the single select.
Edit on category opens the categories list showing only the categories that have a limit, /categories?flag=limited, with the removable tag "Has a limit" (5d). Each category's limit is set from there. Inside a category crumb the card is that category's alone, and Edit opens its edit page (5f) with the limit field focused.
Each bar under a figure is one step of the period, and the header names the step. The step follows the period: a day for 30 days, Week and Month, a week for Quarter, a month for Year. A custom period takes the smallest of the three that gives 12 bars or fewer. The header ends with · by day, · by week or · by month to match. The dark bar is the step that holds today, drawn to date, so it may be short. A chart always draws every step of its period, so a period that has just started shows as many bars as a past one and paging never changes the count: steps still to come are drawn empty, with no readout and no hover band, and open nothing (data-future).
Limits draws only the categories with spend in the period. A limit is an upper limit, not a target, so a category with a limit and no spend is not drawn. The card has no foot line: limits belong to one category each and are never added together. When no category with a limit has spend, the card reads "Nothing has been spent in a category with a limit.", in the same place as the text for no limits: under the card's title, in the small grey type (sp-meta). Inside one category's crumb the card is that category's and is always drawn, spend or not.
Limits holds to the list cap in 4d. It rests at its top 8 rows and one "Other N categories" line with their total. Show top P of N opens it to P rows, P being Rows per page, and under them one line reads "Top P of N. Edit on category lists them all." With P or fewer rows the button reads "Show all N".
"Ahead of pace" shows when the share used is more than 10 points over the pace - Lighting at 91% against 65%, not Seating at 72%. The same rule holds in the category list (5d).
The drill path is the Showing card under the period row (4b). Its head holds the name, Reset and Open in orders; the path sits below the head and wraps there, so the head stays one line at every width and on a phone (7a). At rest there are no crumbs and no card.
The pointed column carries a full-height --hover band, and the readout stands in it, so it reads as that month's however tall the bars beside it are. Its head is the month as a word with a capital and the year, "January 2026", in every locale. The same holds for every month name that stands alone or starts a label - the period crumb, chart ticks, row labels - Danish included. Inside running text a month follows the language.
The monthly limit is named in the legend, beside the series, with a dashed amber key. The dashed line in the plot carries no label of its own, so no bar is ever covered by one.
Spend by month's readout stands above the bar it describes, centred on it, 8px clear of the top of the tallest of that bar and its two neighbours, so it covers none of the three. Near the chart's left or right end its edge sits on the bar's outer edge. It is never held inside the plot or the card: where there is no room above the bars, it stands above the plot, as August here (15f).
Hovering a bar shows its readout, the same .sp-tip--panel as Spend by month without the category rows: the step and its value in the head, the step's order count beneath. The bars are not buttons and do not drill in; the chart below does that for the same steps, and keyboard and screen-reader users read the figures there.
Saved compares amounts, like the other three. The pill is the change in kroner against the previous period, the meta line that period's saving. Never a percentage.

4b Arriving from a "See in Statistics" link
Every vendor and category panel carries one. It opens this page with that record already applied as a crumb - the same state as clicking its bar on the resting page, so there is one code path, not two.
Notes for implementation
The link is a URL, not a handler. /statistics?period=2026&crumbs=vendor:indbo - so it is linkable, survives reload, and the browser back button returns to the panel. A click that pushes state without touching the URL cannot do any of those.
The crumb row appears only when there is at least one crumb. On the resting page (4a) it is absent, not an empty strip - nothing is being narrowed, so there is nothing to say.
Every card re-filters to the crumb, including the KPIs. A card that cannot express the filter is not left showing unfiltered numbers - it is removed. Two figures side by side, one filtered and one not, is the worst outcome available here.
KPI deltas change what they compare against. At rest they compare with the previous period; inside a crumb they compare with the parent - "29% of the year", "+91% vs all vendors". The number alone is meaningless without knowing which.
The order table at the bottom is always the orders behind exactly what is shown. It is the answer to "which orders is this number made of", and it is the reason a user trusts the chart above it. Its row component is the order list's, unchanged.
The period control keeps working alongside crumbs. Period and crumbs are independent filters - switching to Quarter narrows the same vendor, it does not drop it.

4c Two crumbs deep, and how a crumb is removed
Crumbs are an ordered stack. Each one narrows what the last one left, and each is independently removable - the last carries the ×, and clicking an earlier one truncates to it.
Notes for implementation
Only the last crumb carries an ×. Earlier ones are clickable to truncate back to that level. Two ways to remove the same thing invites the question of what removing a middle crumb means.
Removing a middle crumb keeps the ones after it if they are still valid, and drops those that are not. Vendor ▸ Decor with the vendor removed is still a valid Decor view; Decor ▸ August with Decor removed leaves August alone.
Drill order is not fixed. Vendor then category and category then vendor reach the same set, and the crumb row reads in the order the user drilled - it is a record of their path, not a fixed hierarchy.
Cards can appear as you drill, not only disappear. Two crumbs in, a category limit becomes meaningful, so the limit-by-month card exists here and not at rest. A card that only makes sense inside a crumb belongs inside it.
Depth is capped at three. Beyond that the order table below is the better answer than another chart of four rows.
The crumb row wraps rather than scrolling, and never truncates a record's name - a crumb the user cannot read is a filter they cannot verify.

4d A year with 200 vendors
A mature account, three years on: 200 vendors and 34 categories in one year. At that scale a breakdown has hundreds of rows, and a 200-row card is unreadable whether it scrolls or not. Every breakdown rests at its top eight with the remainder carried as one row, and expands in place, to at most one page, when asked. Nothing on this page is an open-ended list.
Notes for implementation
No list on Statistics or Home shows more than one page of rows. The page is P, the Rows per page set in Settings (11a), never fewer than 10, and it is the same number for every list. A list rests at its top 8 rows, the rest carried as one line, "Other N categories" or "Other N vendors", with their total. Its button reads "Show top P of N categories" and opens the top P rows, the rest still one Other line; it never opens the whole list. With P or fewer rows it reads "Show all N" and opens them all. Home's cards stay below the page: 3 rows in Needs a look, 4 in Categories this month.
One vendor card everywhere: Top vendors. It rests at its top eight and Other; its meta reads "top 8 of 200" at rest, "top 10 of 200" open (top P of N), and "all N" when every vendor is shown; its footer carries the total beside the button.
Eight rows, then one Other row, then the link. The Other row carries the summed remainder and its count, so the column still adds to the KPI above it. Without it the card silently understates the total and every percentage on it is wrong.
Expansion happens in the card, not on a new page. The button swaps the eight rows for the top P and the Other line; the card keeps its position in the grid and the crumb state is untouched. A separate ranked page would need its own period control, its own crumbs and its own back behaviour.
The Other row is not clickable. Drilling into "192 vendors" has no meaning as a crumb. Open, it carries the remainder past P ("Other 190 vendors"); it goes only when Show all N has shown every row.
Charts cap at ten series plus Other. Beyond ten bars the axis labels collide and the small values fall below a pixel. The cap is on the chart, not the query - the Other bar is the same remainder the ranked list uses.
The order table loads one page (Rows per page) at a time. Load more appends and slides the page to the first new row, as in the list footer (c6); the footer count always names the real total, so the user knows what they have not seen. No page numbers - the table is proof of the slice above it, not something to navigate.
Expanded and loaded state is not persisted. Changing the period or any crumb collapses every card back to eight and the table back to one page (Rows per page), because the rows answer a query that just changed.
No search or sort inside a breakdown. It is ranked by the measure the card is about. A user looking for one named vendor uses the vendor list or search, which are built for that.
A link opens what it counts. A button or row that shows a number opens a list with exactly that many items. If it cannot, the number is not shown.
Which of these change the URL. A vendor row does - it appends a crumb (crumbs=vendor:hay) and every card refilters. The order table's Load more does too, as on every list (?pages=3), so an opened order and Back return to the same rows. Show top P of N, Show all N and Show top 8 only do not: they are card-local view state, so a reload comes back at top 8. An order row leaves the page for /orders/:id; Open in orders leaves for /orders with the crumbs translated into filter chips. The Other row and the Other bar are inert.

4e The "Expenses only" menu
Three options, single choice, because an order is either money out or money back and a user asking about spend means one of three things: what I paid, what came back, or the difference. The control always reads the choice, never the word "Filter".
Notes for implementation
Values: expenses (default) · refunds · net. In the URL as flow=, alongside period and crumbs, so a shared link carries it.
What each value selects. expenses: orders that are Ordered or Delivered. refunds: orders that are Returned or Sold, both money back. With none in the period the page shows the empty state "No returned or sold orders in" and the period. net: both, a return counted against its spend. A returned order is never an expense.
It filters, it does not change definitions. Median order, the saving and every breakdown recompute over the selected set. Under refunds the KPI labels stay the same and the figures are refund figures - no relabelling, or two screenshots of the page cannot be compared.
Under net, negative values are possible in a breakdown row and in a month bar. Rows render with a minus sign in the red token; bars draw downward from the axis. A card that cannot express a negative (the donut) is removed at that setting rather than drawn with a false slice.
The menu sits in the top bar with Export, at its right end, not in the period row. The period row holds the period alone, so its presets keep their width at every desktop width.
The choice survives crumb and period changes and is not reset by them. It is a lens on the data, not part of the drill path, so it never appears as a crumb.
Budget limits only apply under expenses and net. A limit compared against refunds is meaningless, so the limit cards and the pace marker come out under refunds.
Not a multi-select. Two of three checked is either net or a single value, so checkboxes would offer a state that has no answer.

4f Custom - the range picker
What the sixth preset opens. The five named windows resolve to a range on their own; Custom is the one that asks, so the control grows a panel under it rather than sending the user to another screen. Everything in it is c14's period control unchanged - the quick ranges, the two date fields, two months of days, and the count of what is selected.
Notes for implementation
Nothing recomputes until Apply. The panel is a draft: picking days changes the fields and the count, and the page behind it is untouched. Cancel closes it and leaves the period as it was. This is the one control on the page that does not filter as you touch it, because a half-entered range is not a range.
The quick ranges are shortcuts to a range, not presets of their own. This month, Last month, Year to date and All time each fill both fields and leave the panel open, so the user can still adjust before applying. They are not a second set of windows - the five named presets are the windows.
Two months, always. A range crosses a month boundary more often than not, and one month means paging back and forth to see both ends. The arrows move the pair together.
