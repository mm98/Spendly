R React components
The components in react_handoff/, rendered from their own source files rather than redrawn. Every specimen below is a real call to a real component, so a prop set that has drifted from the library shows up here as a broken specimen rather than as a surprise in the app.
Notes for implementation
What moves, and how fast. One pace, the --motion-* tokens in tokens.css, read by CSS and by Motion alike. CSS keeps what works in every browser: hover and colour, check boxes and radios, the chevrons' turn, submenus, gauges and bars, the calendar's month slide, the password shuffle. Motion (motion.js) runs what CSS cannot: a part leaving the page, a marker or a row moving to a new place, height folds and reordering. A page switch is not animated: the new page replaces the old at once, so the body is never blank for a frame and nothing on it remounts. motion.js reads the tokens off :root once and turns them into Motion's units, so no number is written twice. MotionShell goes once around the app and the Storybook decorator: LazyMotion with domMax, loaded with the app (layoutId, layout and drag need it from the first page switch, and a part closed straight away still runs its way out), and MotionConfig reducedMotion="user", so a reader who asks for less motion gets fades only, everywhere, and no component checks for itself. On this page m is the full motion, since each specimen renders in its own root.
This page is a harness, not a build. It fetches each .jsx file, strips the module syntax, and evaluates it against React with three stand-ins: the router's link, the app's Icon, and the shared variant helpers. The app compiles the same files properly; nothing here is a second implementation of them.
A console error is the point. The components report an unknown prop value, a className or a style rather than rendering something wrong quietly. The last block on this page calls them wrongly on purpose, so the reporting is visible in the browser console.
The stylesheets are the ones the pages use. No copy, no override: tokens.css then components.css, loaded from styles/.
Every specimen sits on the surface it sits on in the product. Not decoration: the focus ring is --ring, three pixels of #e7efe8, and against the page ground it is almost the same colour as the page. A field drawn loose on the page therefore looked as though it had no ring at all, when the ring was there and the specimen was wrong. Controls live inside a card, rows live inside a card or a list, and the specimens do too.

Numbers are written the reader's way. The amount reads 1.320,00 because a native number input cannot render a comma separator - its value is always dot-decimal - so an amount is a text input with a decimal input mode, formatted on blur and accepting either separator while typing. The value stays a number; only the text is localised. The specimens pass da-DK; with no locale they follow the browser.
A native control's own glyphs are the only ones in the design that are not from the register. The search field's clear is removed outright. The date field's calendar cannot be: in current Chromium it survives display: none, a cleared background and a zero width, so it is covered instead - the field keeps its ordinary padding, which puts the glyph in the field's own trailing slot, and the trail button sits over it on the field's ground carrying the register's calendar. That button opens the field's calendar: the library's own on desktop, the native one on a phone (next note).
A date is drawn on desktop and native on touch, and the surface decides. On a phone the platform's picker beats anything drawn - a full-screen wheel, the reader's format, the keyboard they expect. On a desktop it is a small dark dropdown nobody can style, so the field opens the library's own calendar. The field looks for a .sp-touch surface above it rather than asking the caller which it is.
The calendar takes the keyboard. One month grid, used three times - the date field's dropdown, the range's two months, the phone's sheet. It is a real grid: rows of gridcells with one day in the tab order, so the arrow keys walk the month instead of the reader tabbing through 31 buttons. Arrows move a day and a week, PageUp and PageDown a month, with Shift a year, Home and End the ends of the week; Enter picks, Escape closes and returns focus to the trigger, and clicking away closes it. Every day carries its full date for a screen reader, today is aria-current, and a day outside min or max is disabled rather than missing. The weekday order comes from the locale.

r6 ListItem variant · color · mark · handle · actions · count · chevron
A row that opens and has a handle is a plain box with the handle and the open target side by side, as Components, Ordering by hand, draws it. The open target, sp-list-item__open, holds the mark, the count and the words and stretches over the row; actions and the handle sit above it. NavItem the same.
Motion. A list whose rows change wraps them, keyed, in <Rows count={n}>: rows loaded with the list stay still, a row added later drops in, a row removed fades where it stood and the rows below glide up, and after a sort or a filter each row glides to its new place. Past 60 rows nothing glides. A list the reader orders by hand is a ReorderList (Reorder.jsx, Motion's Reorder): the handle carries a row and the rows it passes make room; a press picks it up for the arrows, Space puts it down, Escape puts it back, and each move is read out. Its rows arrive and leave as any list's do: it holds its own Rows inside Reorder.Group, so the page puts none around it, and a moving row passes useRow() to itemOf. While a row is carried the rows it passes make room, it tilts 0.6 degrees (the rotate property, since the drag writes its own transform), and the 2px insertion line stands just above the place it will land, as Components, Ordering by hand, draws it. A pointer drags once it has moved 4px.
One row for every list in the product. It was two components - one for an editable set, one for everything else - until the two were compared: they differed by one shade of ground and 2px of radius. What varies is what the row carries, not the row.
A surface is not a row. A Card owns a ground, an edge and padding and holds a section of a page; its row variant is layout only, laying its own children in a line. A row that wants a card's ground is this component's card variant, because it repeats - and repeating is what separates a row from a surface. The control states, the text column and the line parts live here, not on the card. Which body a list shows is a question about the list's own width, answered by a container query - one implementation, no phone-only component.

r7 Menu, Popover, Dialog three promises, not three sizes
Motion. In a set of radio rows the green tint is one marker that moves from the old row to the new. A submenu slides out from its item, its items following 20ms apart, in CSS. A dialog grows the last 2% into place over a fading scrim, and a popover drops in from its trigger; both leave the same way under Leaving.
Closing with the fade. The page keeps its condition and wraps it: <Leaving onClosed={...}>{open ? <Dialog ...> : null}</Leaving>. Leaving is Motion's AnimatePresence under the same name and props, so its 63 uses do not change: when the child goes, its way out runs, it takes no more presses meanwhile, and onClosed is called once it has gone.
A Menu acts on click: every item does its thing and closes. A Popover waits - it holds a decision and a Cancel / Apply pair, so nothing happens until the reader applies it. A Dialog interrupts, and cannot be answered by ignoring it. That is why they are three components.

r9 List records · columns · bulkActions
Motion. The List wraps its own table rows and record cards in Rows, so a row added drops in, a row removed fades and the rest glide up, and a sort or a filter glides each row to its new place, as ListItem does (r6).
Written the way a table is written. A Caption says what it is, a ListHead of cells declares each column's heading and width, and a ListRow is the template every record is drawn through - its child is a function of one record, returning that record's cells in the head's order. That order is the one thing this shape asks of a page, so the list checks it: a row drawing a different number of cells from the head is reported, because every cell after the gap would otherwise sit under the wrong heading in silence.
The page supplies the data, the head, the row and what can be done to a selection. Everything else is the list's, because it is the same on every list: the count, the caption becoming the bulk bar, the select-all box and its three states, shift-click taking the run, Escape clearing, the pager, and paging keeping a selection where a filter change drops it. Selected is the only reason a list has boxes - leave it out and the list is a plain table.
A head cell states a role, never a width: seven roles, each with its width declared once in the stylesheet, and exactly one title - the column with no width of its own, which absorbs whatever the fixed ones leave. That is why the title looks different on every page and every other column is identical everywhere.

r12 Shell, Sidebar the page's own furniture
Motion. The current row's ground slides from the row it left to the row it reached (NavGround, with Motion's animate and glide()), in the dark and the light sidebar alike. The labels run in from --motion-slide as the column opens and out before it closes; Sidebar calls onMotionEnd when they are done, which is when the shell narrows the column. A page switch is not animated: the new page replaces the old at once inside ShellContent, and the sidebar and top bar stay still.
A page scroll never moves the marker. A layoutId marker is measured on the page, and the old one is measured while the new page is being prepared, before it scrolls to the top, so a sidebar that stays put read a page scroll as movement. NavItem draws its ground with NavGround instead: it keeps the row the ground was last on and where on it, reads that row and the new row at the same moment, and slides by the difference (y and scaleY, from the top). It slides only between rows of one box, the nearest .sp-scrolls or .sp-nav-list: from the scrolling list to the pinned rows, or from Settings' sections to the Views card, the ground is drawn at once. Nothing in the sidebar or the light lists uses layoutRoot, layoutScroll or a LayoutGroup. Segmented, PeriodPicker and Choice keep layoutId: they move with the page.
The account row. SidebarAccount is a NavItem whose whole row is the one button that opens the account menu, as it was: the photo or initials as its mark, the name over the address as its label, the up chevron as its trail. It keeps aria-haspopup="menu", aria-expanded, Up and Down to open the menu at either end, and the menu placed above the row at its width, or beside the mark on the rail. Its focus ring is the nav row's own. Its name is the words drawn on it, the name and the address; the mark is hidden from screen readers. No control: that is a Switch, and makes the row a label.
The shell is a grid of two tracks, and collapsing it is a track width rather than an animation - an animated track reflows every card beside it on every frame. The rail is the same sidebar at its narrow width, and peeking shows its labels over the page rather than pushing it.
