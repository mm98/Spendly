11a Settings
Thirteen panes, one per thing that changes how the rest of the app behaves. A second navigation inside the page lists them, and the pane itself is one column of cards at a reading width - not the list-and-rail split the record screens use, because nothing here is being looked up against anything. Nothing saves by itself: each card has its own save, and only the card you touched goes dirty.
Notes for implementation
A very long status name ends in an ellipsis in this list (.sp-pill--truncate), so its row keeps its height; the full name is in the edit field. This is the one place a pill truncates.
Passkeys is a card under Account and profile. Add a passkey opens the device's prompt (iPhone, Android, 1Password); the new row takes the name the device reports, which Rename changes. Rename turns the row into its name field with Cancel and Save, drawn on the 1Password row, the same as renaming a status; Enter saves and Escape cancels. Remove asks first with the confirm dialog, and the password always keeps working, so removing the last one locks nobody out. With none, the card holds only its line and Add a passkey. Adding one sends the passkey-added email (20f).
Two-step sign-in is its own card right under Passkeys, drawn here on. Every state and its three dialogs are in 11f.
Delete account is the last card in Account and profile, after Two-step sign-in. The card and every state of its dialog are in 11g.
Passkeys: Rename does not move the card. A passkey row is one height at rest and while renaming: the name field's 42px (--control-h). At rest the row holds that height with the name and its date line centred in it; on Rename the field, Cancel and Save fill it exactly, so the rows and rules below stay where they are. Lock the row's height, not its content: a row sized by its text grows by 3px when the field appears.
Status email is its own card in Account and profile, right under the account card: two Toggles with a description, Weekly and Monthly (c5, .sp-choice--described), the control Needs a look uses, either, both or neither. Both off means no status email, the default for every account. Each saves on change, with no Save button. Both are drawn on here to show they can be picked together. When the 1st is a Monday and both are on, only the monthly email is sent that day. Stop these emails in the email turns both off.
Send a test email sends the email for the period just ended to the account's address, now, with the account's real figures, in one frequency: the weekly one when Weekly is on or when both are off, the monthly one when only Monthly is on. The subject starts "Test:". While it sends the button reads Sending... with the spinner and is disabled, as Check for updates does (11d); then a toast says "Test sent to mikkel@example.net". On failure the toast is the danger toast: "The test could not be sent. Try again." The button is not rate-limited in the drawing; the server allows one test a minute.
Limit alerts is its own pane, between Lists and Statuses: the in-app switches and "Tell me early, at" (8b).
Needs a look is its own pane, after Limit alerts: one switch per row Home's Needs a look card can show (10a), in the order Home ranks them. Off removes that row from Home and from the phone's Home; the cap of three applies to what is left, and with every switch off the card is absent. Orders without a receipt is off by default, since most orders have none. It saves with the card's own save, like every card here.
AI assistants is its own pane, after Statuses: the tokens an assistant program reads this account with. Its card is drawn here at rest with two tokens; every other state is in 11e.
Compare this month with sits in Appearance and offers Last month (the default) and Same month last year. It sets what Home's month card (10a), the phone's Home and the monthly summary (8b) measure against. It saves with the card's own save, like every card here.
Colour scheme sits in Appearance under Theme: 17 schemes, Green the default, four tiles to a row. Each tile shows the scheme as its tones: the sidebar, then the primary scale from darkest to lightest, in the theme in use. A scheme replaces the primary scale, the neutrals and the sidebar (styles/schemes.css, set as data-scheme on the root beside data-theme) and holds for Light, Dark and Follow system. Ink, amber and red are the same in every scheme. Savings and positive figures take the scheme's primary. It saves with the card's own save and is stored on the account, so the emails use it too (20f).
The language and region setting writes dates, times and numbers - the preview shows all three - and every figure in the app follows it, amounts included.
The profile photo is a FileDrop: drop an image on the round mark, or use Upload a photo. With no photo the mark is the initials on --green-3, round, as in the sidebar.
Draft, Ordered, Delivered, Returned and Cancelled are built in and are never deleted, whatever their order count; Delivered is drawn mid-rename as Received. The app knows Returned and Cancelled by what they mean, not by their name: they are left out of spending, a scanned return receipt is filed under Returned, demo data and imports use them, and a cancelled order is struck out. A new status with the same name would not bring that back, so the row has no delete control. Each built-in row carries the "built in" pill; all but Draft keep the pencil, since a rename changes only the name.
Statuses on a phone (STATUSES · PHONE): each row is the handle, the name's pill, "built in" where it applies, and one actions button with Rename, and Delete on an added status with no orders. The order count is left out on a phone, so the pill sits in the middle of the row with the rest; a long name ends in an ellipsis. The pencil and the trash are desktop only: side by side they sit 36px apart on a phone, too close for two 44px targets. Draft has neither, so it has no actions button.
Renaming on a phone wraps the row: the name field on the first line, with no drag handle while the row is renamed, the four tones on the second, Cancel and Save at the right on the third. The row grows while it is being renamed and returns to its height on Save or Cancel.
A status the reader added can be deleted once no orders use it. The delete control is absent on a row with orders, not disabled - reassigning the orders is what makes it appear. Every row except Draft has the pencil; an added status with no orders also has the trash. Rename, reorder and delete all wait for the save bar, like every change on this page. Its tone is one of the four the pill component has, picked as the pill the list will draw.
Order views in the menu offers 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 - same numeric-combobox pattern as Rows per page (tabular figures, unit word beside the value, chevron right). Views beyond the chosen count collapse into the "N more in Orders" row in the sidebar. The list is always the first N; when the open view is one of the rest, that row is marked current and nothing else changes (9e).
The save bar is sticky to the bottom of the content column. Each card saves on its own, so the bar names which one is dirty - and with a pane this long the dirty card is usually scrolled off screen by the time you want to save it. A bar that scrolls away leaves the only exit from an edit somewhere the user has to hunt for. It appears when something is dirty and is absent otherwise, matching the edit screen in 1d.
The section nav and its divider stay put; only the pane scrolls. The nav column is exactly the height of the window below the top bar (position: sticky; top: 0 inside the scroll area, with that height, or a fixed column beside it) and carries the 1px --line-3 divider on its right edge. The divider always reaches the bottom of the window, whether the pane is shorter than the window or scrolled. A divider drawn on an element that scrolls with the pane, or one sized to the pane's content, scrolls away or ends partway down.
The confirm-email banner sits in the pane column, above the first card. Drawn here for an account that has not confirmed its address; a confirmed account has no banner and the first card moves up. The banner is the warning row of 20e and takes the pane's width and padding, so the section nav beside it and its divider still start at the top bar. It scrolls with the pane, as on every page. Placing it above both columns would start the divider under the banner, the fault later.md records.
The section nav's ground slides from section to section while the pane scrolls. The nav stays put, so its ground is the sidebar's NavGround, which reads the row left and the row picked at the same moment: picking a section scrolls the pane to it and the ground moves straight to the row picked. A ground slides only within one sp-nav-list, so leaving Settings for Orders does not glide it into the Views card; it is drawn there at once.
Note on the frame
The 1180px shell below is presentation only - a stand-in for a viewport, so the screen reads in context on this page. It is not a width the app has. In the app the shell fills the browser edge to edge: sidebar 248px fixed, content column fluid, exactly as 9a defines it.
Inside the content column, three widths do the work: the section nav is 208px fixed, the divider beside it is a hairline, and the card column is fluid with a 640px cap. Past 640px the cards stop growing and the slack falls to their right - a 42px input stretched across a 1920px screen looks like an error (15a). Below roughly 900px the section nav becomes a select above the cards, and the cap stops binding because the column is already narrower than it.

    Notes for implementation
    Every line in Previous imports ends in its menu (c9, the same trigger and panel as a table row's actions): Reuse mapping, and Remove from this list on a line older than 24 hours. An import from the last 24 hours also offers Undo as a button before the menu, with the same effect as Undo this import; drawn on webshop-orders.csv. After an undo its facts end with "undone".
    Undo asks first, from either place. Undo this import (stage 4) and Undo in Previous imports both open "Undo this import?" (c13, 6d); nothing is deleted at the click. The body names the file, the import date, the orders and item lines it wrote, and the vendors and categories it created that nothing else uses now; a part with a count of zero is left out. The line about changed orders appears only when at least one order was edited after the import, with that count. The button carries the order count. Both Undo buttons stay outline, as any button that opens a dialog.
    An import is unsaved changes from the moment a file is chosen until Import N orders is pressed. Leaving in stages 2 or 3 - another page, the sidebar, Back, closing the tab - asks "Leave without saving?", the same dialog as an edited order or a dirty Settings card. Keep editing stays on the stage with every choice kept; Leave discards the file and the mapping, and the import starts again at stage 1. Cancel in stage 3 is the same as Leave and asks the same question. Stage 1 and stage 4 ask nothing: nothing is chosen yet, or it is already written. The mapping is saved for Reuse mapping only when an import is written.
    Remove from this list is the second item in the menu of a line older than 24 hours. It hides the line from Previous imports only: the orders stay as they are and keep their link to the import, so the order list's import chip (2b) still filters to them, and a hidden import's chip still reads its file date. The toast offers Undo for its usual time. A line inside its 24 hours has no Remove: it can be removed once its Undo has gone, so a line never hides its own Undo and nothing waits in between. The server keeps a hidden flag on the import and leaves hidden imports out of the list; reading one import by id ignores the flag.
    A faded row is drawn at full strength while its field list is open (.sp-muted:has([data-open="true"])), and fades again when the list closes. A fade thins the list it holds and makes the row a layer of its own, which the rows after it draw over. The same holds for a cancelled order: its row (2b) and its card (2c) are at full strength while the order's menu is open. On a phone the list opens as a sheet, outside the row, so nothing changes there.

    Notes for implementation
    The pickers and the warning are not shown at rest. The card opens as one secondary button. Pressing it reveals the two choices, the warning and the confirm pair - so a destructive action takes two deliberate presses without a dialog, and the settings page never carries a permanent red panel.
    Filling is determinate. The count is known before it starts, so the bar shows real progress and a figure. There is no cancel: a half-filled account is the failure state, and stopping on purpose would produce it deliberately.
    Done and Failed both say what now exists. Failure keeps the partial rows rather than rolling back, and says how many - the user needs to know what is in their lists, and this is demo data, so a partial set costs nothing.
    Red here is correct. This is the one control in Settings that destroys data, which is why the warning is red rather than amber and why the confirm names the consequence instead of saying OK.

    Notes for implementation
    Five states, one card. Resting shows what is installed and offers the check. Checking disables the button and swaps its label for a verb. Up to date and Update available both answer the question that was asked, so the result sits in the card rather than in a toast - a toast would be gone before the user looked back. The notice that a newer version is running on the server (6d) is a separate case: nobody asked for it, so it comes to the user and stays until answered. Check failed keeps the version line intact, because a failed check says nothing about what is running.
    Checking holds for at least 2 seconds, even when the answer arrives sooner. A check that returns in 80ms flashes the spinner and lands on a result the user did not see appear - it reads as the button having done nothing. Hold the state, then swap. The version line stays in place throughout. Its hint shows at rest and while checking; a result takes the hint's place, at the same height, so nothing below the card moves.
    Only state 4 has a primary button. Updating is the one action here with a consequence, and it is the only state where the button changes what it does rather than repeating the check.
    The result does not persist. States 3 to 5 hold until the card leaves the screen, then the card returns to resting. A stale "up to date" is worse than no answer.
    The version number. Three numbers, such as 1.4.2: major, minor and build. A big feature raises major, a merged change raises minor, and every build raises build; each raise sets the parts after it to 0. It sits in the version line in bold ink, the same as the date, in every state; the short commit stays last in faded ink. A build with no version number (an older image) shows the line without it: "You're running the version installed on 18.08.2026, 09.14 (a3f91c2)."
