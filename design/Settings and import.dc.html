11a Settings
Thirteen panes, one per thing that changes how the rest of the app behaves, in two groups: You, which only changes the app for you, and Shared, which changes it for everyone in the household. A second navigation inside the page lists them, and the pane itself is one column of cards at a reading width - not the list-and-rail split the record screens use, because nothing here is being looked up against anything. Nothing saves by itself: each card has its own save, and only the card you touched goes dirty.
Notes for implementation
A very long status name ends in an ellipsis in this list (.sp-pill--truncate), so its row keeps its height; the full name is in the edit field. This is the one place a pill truncates.
Passkeys is a card under Account and profile. Add a passkey opens the device's prompt (iPhone, Android, 1Password); the new row takes the name the device reports, which Rename changes. Remove asks first with the confirm dialog, and the password always keeps working, so removing the last one locks nobody out. With none, the card holds only its line and Add a passkey. Adding one sends the passkey-added email (20f).
People with access is the first pane in Shared. The data model: orders, vendors, categories, lists, statuses, limits, imports and attachments belong to a household, not to a user; a user is a login with personal settings; a membership joins one user to one household with a role, owner or member. Registering (20a) creates the household with that person as its owner, so a single-person account is a household of one and nothing in the UI names it.
Inviting. Send invite checks the address on submit, adds a pending row at the end of the list and sends the invite email (20f). The link works for 7 days and once. Send again sends a fresh link and resets the 7 days; Cancel invite removes the row and the link stops working. An expired invite stays as a row reading "Invite expired" with Send again and Cancel invite. Inviting an address that is already a member, or already pending, shows the field error "Already in your list".
Owner and member. Both can do everything with the data and both can invite. Only the owner removes a person; a member's view of this card has no Remove buttons, and their own row has Leave instead. Remove and Leave ask first with the confirm dialog (6d). A removed person loses access at once and their orders stay, still showing their name as who added them. The owner cannot leave or be removed; deleting the account deletes the household for everyone.
Build roles as permissions, so more can come later. The membership stores a role, and the role maps to a set of named permissions (manage people, delete orders, edit shared settings, import, and so on). The server and the UI check a permission, never the role name: code that reads can('manage_people') keeps working when a third role arrives, code that reads role === 'owner' does not. Today owner has every permission and member has all but manage people. An action the user lacks is absent, not disabled, as with the delete control on a status that has orders.
Shared and personal settings. The two groups in the section nav are the split. You, per user: Account and profile (with Passkeys), Appearance (theme, row density, compare this month with), Dates, times and numbers, Limit alerts, Needs a look. Shared, per household: People with access, Currency, Defaults, Lists, Statuses, Import, Demo data. A change to a shared card applies to everyone at once, with the same save as today. This build sits outside both. The profile row at the foot of the sidebar opens Settings on Account and profile. Below roughly 900px the section nav becomes a select, and the two group names become its option groups.
Permissions, when they come, live in Shared. Assigning a role is a picker on each row of People with access, shown to whoever may manage people. Defining roles, if custom roles are ever added, is one more pane, Roles, directly after People with access, each role a list of permission switches in the Needs a look pattern.
Who added what appears only with more than one person. In a household of one there is no Added by facet on the order list (2b), no Added by row on the order (1a) and no name on Scanned before (3c). They appear when a second person joins and stay while there are two or more. A pending invite does not count. With only you in the list, this card shows your row and the invite field.
Limit alerts is its own pane in You, after Dates, times and numbers: the in-app switches and "Tell me early, at" (8b).
Needs a look is its own pane, after Limit alerts: one switch per row Home's Needs a look card can show (10a), in the order Home ranks them. Off removes that row from Home and from the phone's Home; the cap of three applies to what is left, and with every switch off the card is absent. Orders without a receipt is off by default, since most orders have none. It saves with the card's own save, like every card here.
Compare this month with sits in Appearance and offers Last month (the default) and Same month last year. It sets what Home's month card (10a), the phone's Home and the monthly summary (8b) measure against. It saves with the card's own save, like every card here.
The language and region setting writes dates, times and numbers - the preview shows all three - and every figure in the app follows it, amounts included.
The profile photo is a FileDrop: drop an image on the round mark, or use Upload a photo. With no photo the mark is the initials on --green-3, round, as in the sidebar.
A status with orders on it cannot be deleted. The delete control is absent on those rows, not disabled - reassigning the orders is what makes it appear. Every row has the pencil; a row with no orders also has the trash. Rename, reorder and delete all wait for the save bar, like every change on this page. Its tone is one of the four the pill component has, picked as the pill the list will draw.
Order views in the menu offers 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 - same numeric-combobox pattern as Rows per page (tabular figures, unit word beside the value, chevron right). Views beyond the chosen count collapse into the "N more in Orders" row in the sidebar. The list is always the first N; when the open view is one of the rest, that row is marked current and nothing else changes (9e).
The save bar is sticky to the bottom of the content column. Each card saves on its own, so the bar names which one is dirty - and with a pane this long the dirty card is usually scrolled off screen by the time you want to save it. A bar that scrolls away leaves the only exit from an edit somewhere the user has to hunt for. It appears when something is dirty and is absent otherwise, matching the edit screen in 1d.
Note on the frame
The 1180px shell below is presentation only - a stand-in for a viewport, so the screen reads in context on this page. It is not a width the app has. In the app the shell fills the browser edge to edge: sidebar 248px fixed, content column fluid, exactly as 9a defines it.
Inside the content column, three widths do the work: the section nav is 208px fixed, the divider beside it is a hairline, and the card column is fluid with a 640px cap. Past 640px the cards stop growing and the slack falls to their right - a 42px input stretched across a 1920px screen looks like an error (15a). Below roughly 900px the section nav becomes a select above the cards, and the cap stops binding because the column is already narrower than it.
The divider beside the section nav always reaches the bottom of the window. The nav column does not scroll: it is exactly the height of the window below the top bar (position: sticky; top: 0; height: 100% of the scroll area, or a fixed column beside it), and it carries the 1px --line-3 divider on its right edge. Only the pane scrolls. A divider drawn on an element that scrolls with the pane, or one sized to the pane's content, ends where the last card ends once the pane is scrolled to its foot - the line stops above the menu's own last entries. If the menu is taller than the window, the nav column scrolls on its own, and the divider still runs its full height.

Notes for implementation
The pickers and the warning are not shown at rest. The card opens as one secondary button. Pressing it reveals the two choices, the warning and the confirm pair - so a destructive action takes two deliberate presses without a dialog, and the settings page never carries a permanent red panel.
Filling is determinate. The count is known before it starts, so the bar shows real progress and a figure. There is no cancel: a half-filled account is the failure state, and stopping on purpose would produce it deliberately.
Done and Failed both say what now exists. Failure keeps the partial rows rather than rolling back, and says how many - the user needs to know what is in their lists, and this is demo data, so a partial set costs nothing.
Red here is correct. This is the one control in Settings that destroys data, which is why the warning is red rather than amber and why the confirm names the consequence instead of saying OK.

Notes for implementation
Five states, one card. Resting shows what is installed and offers the check. Checking disables the button and swaps its label for a verb. Up to date and Update available both answer the question that was asked, so the result sits in the card rather than in a toast - a toast would be gone before the user looked back. The notice that a newer version is running on the server (6d) is a separate case: nobody asked for it, so it comes to the user and stays until answered. Check failed keeps the version line intact, because a failed check says nothing about what is running.
Checking holds for at least 2 seconds, even when the answer arrives sooner. A check that returns in 80ms flashes the spinner and lands on a result the user did not see appear - it reads as the button having done nothing. Hold the state, then swap. The version line stays in place throughout. Its hint shows at rest and while checking; a result takes the hint's place, at the same height, so nothing below the card moves.
Only state 4 has a primary button. Updating is the one action here with a consequence, and it is the only state where the button changes what it does rather than repeating the check.
The result does not persist. States 3 to 5 hold until the card leaves the screen, then the card returns to resting. A stale "up to date" is worse than no answer.
Amber informs, it does not block. An available update is a fact, not a warning: nothing about the app stops working, and the user may keep the version they have.
